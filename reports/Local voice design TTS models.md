# Design voices with Qwen3-TTS, clone with Echo

As of late November 2026, the best open-weight, locally runnable model for Yakkinator's voice-design step is **Qwen3-TTS VoiceDesign (`Qwen/Qwen3-TTS-12Hz-1.7B-VoiceDesign`)**. It is Apache-2.0 and not reported as gated. It takes a free-text description through a one-line Python API (`generate_voice_design(text, language, instruct)`), runs on native Windows once you drop flash-attn in favour of PyTorch SDPA, and fits in roughly 6–8 GB of VRAM in bf16. It also has the strongest *independently tabulated* English instruction-following score of any open model: 73.5 on InstructTTSEval-EN, against 64.9–65.1 for the next open models in a September 2026 third-party table ([StepAudio 3 Gen report](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.12945)). Three other models are worth knowing about. **VoxCPM2** (Apache-2.0, 48 kHz, `pip install voxcpm`) is the runner-up, best when you want maximum fidelity in the reference clip. **FireRedTTS3-Instruct** (Apache-2.0, Aug 2026) claims higher prompt adherence than Qwen, but it has almost no Windows track record. **Breeze TTS 2** sounds the most natural (top open-weight model on the Artificial Analysis arena), but its non-commercial weights and Linux-only official support make it a poor default. Nearly every benchmark here is vendor-reported, and nobody has measured how well a designed voice survives cloning by a different engine, so build the feature around "generate several candidates, audition, freeze a WAV". Finally, licensing is decided at the end of the pipeline: **Echo-TTS weights and every clip it generates are CC-BY-NC-SA-4.0**, so a permissive designer does not make Echo output commercially usable ([jordandare/echo-tts](https://github.com/jordandare/echo-tts)).

## Only six or seven open models truly turn a text prompt into a new voice

Many popular "expressive" TTS models cannot do this job. **Chatterbox, Dia2, Higgs Audio v2/v3, VibeVoice, Kani-TTS-2, IndexTTS-2/2.5, CosyVoice 3 and Step-Audio-EditX all need reference audio or preset speakers.** Their style or emotion instructions change *how* an existing voice speaks; they cannot create a new timbre. IndexTTS 2.5's text control, for example, routes through a separate QwenEmotion model for emotion only ([IndexTTS 2.5 report](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.03888)). Higgs v2's scene description covers only the acoustic environment ([README_V2](https://github.com/boson-ai/higgs-audio/blob/main/README_V2.md)). Spark-TTS offers only gender, pitch and rate knobs ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/SparkAudio/Spark-TTS-0.5B)). This matters for Yakkinator because a design model is the missing piece: Echo-TTS and IndexTTS-2 are both cloners.

The real description-to-voice models fall into three groups. **Chinese labs:** Qwen3-TTS VoiceDesign (Jan 2026; the 1.7B is the only size with VoiceDesign) ([QwenLM/Qwen3-TTS](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-TTS)), VoxCPM2 (Apr 2026, description in parentheses before the text) ([OpenBMB/VoxCPM](https://github.com/OpenBMB/VoxCPM)), MOSS-VoiceGenerator, which is explicitly pitched as "a voice design layer for other TTS systems" ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/OpenMOSS-Team/MOSS-VoiceGenerator)), FireRedTTS3-Instruct (Aug 2026, plans voice attributes in text before synthesizing) ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/FireRedTeam/FireRedTTS3)), and Ant Group's Ming-3.0-tts-0.5B ([AlphaSignal](https://alphasignal.ai/news/ant-group-s-ming-3-0-tops-open-source-voice-charts-with-five-speech-models)). **Western and independent labs:** Breeze TTS 2 ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/BreezeBlue/Breeze-TTS-2)) and Maya1 ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/maya-research/maya1)). **Legacy:** Parler-TTS, a 2024 model whose control is coarse (gender, pitch, rate, reverb, noise) ([GitHub](https://github.com/huggingface/parler-tts)). The best-scoring voice-design system in the newest benchmark, **StepAudio 3 Gen, is API-only with no weights**, so it is out of scope for a local app ([WaveSpeed guide](https://wavespeed.ai/blog/audio-and-voice-models/stepaudio-3-model-guide/)).

Accent is a blind spot across the board. Model cards list gender, age, timbre, emotion and pace, but none of the sources reliably documents accent control such as "British" or "Australian". The Chinese-first training of most candidates makes English accent rendering the likeliest weak point. Test your own target prompts before promising this in the UI.

## Qwen3-TTS leads on prompt adherence, Breeze on raw naturalness

Two kinds of evidence exist, and they point to different winners. On **prompt adherence** (InstructTTSEval: acoustic-parameter, descriptive-style and role-play tasks, scored by a Gemini judge), Qwen reports EN APS/DSD/RP of **82.9 / 82.4 / 68.4**, ahead of MiMo-Audio-7B, Hume and Parler-large (60.0 / 45.9 / 31.2) ([QwenLM/Qwen3-TTS](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-TTS)). FireRed's table claims FireRedTTS3-Instruct beats Qwen, especially on English role-play (**72.0 vs 64.2**). FireRed is a competitor, though, and its re-scored Qwen numbers are lower than Qwen's own (EN APS 76.4 vs 82.9) ([HF FireRedTTS3](https://huggingface.co/FireRedTeam/FireRedTTS3)). The only roughly neutral table, in StepFun's report, ranks Qwen3-TTS-VD first among open models at 73.5 average, above Ming-omni-tts-0.5B (65.1) and MOSS-VoiceGenerator (64.9) ([StepAudio 3 Gen report](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.12945)). Even this judge methodology has critics: the CapTalk authors say it lacks "sufficient human validation" ([CapTalk](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.08363)).

On **naturalness**, the Artificial Analysis TTS arena (fetched Nov 2026) puts **Breeze TTS 2 at rank 15 (Elo 1230)**, far above Maya1 (1050), Higgs Audio V3 (1035) and Chatterbox (1021) ([Artificial Analysis](https://artificialanalysis.ai/text-to-speech/leaderboard)). "Qwen3 TTS" appears at 932, but that entry is likely the hosted API variant, and the arena scores general TTS, not voice design. Ming-3.0 tops the arena's open-weight "Controlled Voice" board at 1,069 Elo ([AlphaSignal](https://alphasignal.ai/news/ant-group-s-ming-3-0-tops-open-source-voice-charts-with-five-speech-models)). One unconfirmed summary called open-ended voice design a Ming weak spot relative to Qwen. VoxCPM2 and MOSS publish no comparable voice-design numbers. MOSS relies on a 160-sample internal listening test ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/OpenMOSS-Team/MOSS-VoiceGenerator)).

These numbers point to Qwen for this use case. A reference clip only has to carry timbre into Echo-TTS or IndexTTS-2, and their own synthesis then decides the final naturalness. Prompt adherence (getting the voice the user described) therefore matters more than the designer's arena Elo.

Windows support decides the shortlist. The official Qwen example loads with `flash_attention_2`, but community forks agree that **FlashAttention 2 does not build on Windows, and the model runs fine on SDPA, "just slower"** ([Qwen3-TTS-GUI](https://github.com/StroepWafel/Qwen3-TTS-GUI); [Qwen3-TTS-JP](https://github.com/hiroki-abe-58/Qwen3-TTS-JP)). A missing flash-attn produces only a warning ([issue #41](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-TTS/issues/41)). One RTX 5080 user on CUDA 13 could not find matching torch and flash-attn builds ([HF discussion](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-TTS-12Hz-1.7B-CustomVoice/discussions/19)), so pin a CUDA 12.8 torch wheel rather than chasing the newest toolkit. MOSS's hard pins on transformers 5.0.0 and torch 2.9.1 would conflict with the Echo and IndexTTS-2 environments. Yakkinator's one-uv-venv-per-backend design already solves that, but it is a heavier dependency stack for no quality gain. Breeze needs the local-voice project's Inductor workaround on Windows, and its installer is about 23 GB in total ([local-voice](https://github.com/vl3c/local-voice)).

Breeze has one less obvious argument in its favour. Echo-TTS output is already non-commercial, so Breeze's NC terms add little restriction on the Echo path. They would still taint the IndexTTS-2 path, though, and Breeze's own outputs, the designed reference clip included, fall under its license. Add the Linux-first tooling, and Breeze is an optional "high-naturalness" engine at best.

## Recommended setup: Qwen3-TTS in its own uv venv, SDPA, bf16

Add a new FastAPI backend in its own venv, matching the existing pattern. The `qwen-tts` package and Python 3.12 recommendation come from the official repo and PyPI ([QwenLM/Qwen3-TTS](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-TTS); [PyPI qwen-tts](https://pypi.org/project/qwen-tts/)). The explicit cu128 torch install is a precaution against pip pulling a CPU-only wheel on Windows; it is not an official step.

```powershell
uv venv .venv-qwenvd --python 3.12
.\.venv-qwenvd\Scripts\activate
uv pip install torch torchaudio --index-url https://download.pytorch.org/whl/cu128
uv pip install -U qwen-tts soundfile fastapi uvicorn
# Do NOT install flash-attn on Windows; SDPA is used instead.
# Optional pre-download (else fetched on first load):
huggingface-cli download Qwen/Qwen3-TTS-12Hz-1.7B-VoiceDesign
```

The inference call follows the official README, with the attention backend switched to SDPA for Windows ([QwenLM/Qwen3-TTS](https://github.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-TTS)):

```python
import torch, soundfile as sf
from qwen_tts import Qwen3TTSModel

torch.manual_seed(1234)  # log it; reproducibility across GPUs is not guaranteed
model = Qwen3TTSModel.from_pretrained(
    "Qwen/Qwen3-TTS-12Hz-1.7B-VoiceDesign",
    device_map="cuda:0",
    dtype=torch.bfloat16,
    attn_implementation="sdpa",   # flash_attention_2 does not build on Windows
)

script = ("The old lighthouse keeper checked the lamp at dusk, wrote three lines in his log, "
          "and listened to the rain drumming on the iron stairs. Tomorrow, he thought, "
          "would be quieter.")
wavs, sr = model.generate_voice_design(
    text=[script] * 3,            # batch: three candidates for the user to audition
    language=["English"] * 3,
    instruct=["A raspy, middle-aged man with a warm, unhurried baritone and a calm, "
              "matter-of-fact delivery"] * 3,
)
for i, w in enumerate(wavs):
    sf.write(f"candidate_{i}.wav", w, sr)   # sr is returned at runtime (reported 24 kHz)
```

The list-based call relies on the README's statement that the API accepts lists for batching. Whether three identical prompts give three *different* voices depends on sampling, and no source confirms it. If they come out identical, loop over seeds instead. After the user picks a clip, call the app's existing unload endpoint (commit 22b5dd3) to free the GPU for Echo. Echo needs ≥8 GB of its own and offers `FISH_AE_DTYPE=bfloat16` plus a shorter latent length for 8 GB cards ([jordandare/echo-tts](https://github.com/jordandare/echo-tts)). If 48 kHz references become a priority, the fallback is VoxCPM2. Install it with `pip install voxcpm` (Python <3.13), call `VoxCPM.from_pretrained("openbmb/VoxCPM2", load_denoiser=False).generate(text="(description)script", seed=42)`, and write the file at `tts.tts_model.sample_rate`, not the 16 kHz shown in the card snippet ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/openbmb/VoxCPM2/blob/main/README.md)).

## Design-then-clone works if the saved WAV, not the prompt, is the voice

This workflow is officially recommended, not a hack. Qwen's own docs prescribe it: design a short clip, then feed it and its transcript into a cloner as a reusable prompt ([Qwen voice-design guide](https://www.mintlify.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-TTS/guides/voice-design)). MOSS markets itself for exactly this role ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/OpenMOSS-Team/MOSS-VoiceGenerator)). The caveat is that **designed voices drift between runs**. VoxCPM2 says outright that results "may vary between runs" and suggests generating 1–3 times ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/openbmb/VoxCPM2/blob/main/README.md)), and Breeze users see seed-dependent pitch swings from 65–70 Hz to about 95 Hz on the same prompt ([local-voice](https://github.com/vl3c/local-voice)). Store the chosen WAV with its transcript, description, seed and model version, and treat the WAV as the voice's permanent identity. Qwen's cloning guide likewise advises reusing one reference for every generation to keep the speaker stable ([voice-cloning guide](https://mintlify.com/QwenLM/Qwen3-TTS/guides/voice-cloning)).

Aim for a **10–15 s, clean, emotionally neutral, phonetically varied** reference. Third-party guidance says cloning quality rises from about 3 s up to about 15 s and then plateaus ([Better Stack](https://betterstack.com/community/guides/ai/qwen3-tts/)). Echo accepts long references but works well at about 10 s or less ([jordandare/echo-tts](https://github.com/jordandare/echo-tts)). The preview code's 2,560-latent cap works out to roughly 119 s at 44.1 kHz, which contradicts the README's "up to 5 minutes", so do not rely on very long references ([echo-tts-preview inference.py](https://huggingface.co/spaces/jordand/echo-tts-preview/blob/main/inference.py)). IndexTTS-2 needs only "a few seconds" ([IndexTTS2 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.21619v2)). A neutral read keeps the cloner from locking onto one-off emotion. No source tests this for Echo or IndexTTS-2, so treat it as sound practice rather than an established result. For sample rate, save Qwen's native 24 kHz output as lossless WAV. Echo outputs 44.1 kHz, and upsampling the reference first adds no detail. Ask the designer to read a matching transcript, and keep that exact text, because IndexTTS-style and Qwen-style cloners use it.

## Licensing: the designer is permissive, Echo is not

Qwen3-TTS code and weights are Apache-2.0, confirmed by an independent license check on 11 Nov 2026 ([Cekura](https://www.cekura.ai/blogs/open-source-tts)). VoxCPM2, MOSS and FireRedTTS3 are also Apache-2.0, and Ming-3.0 is MIT. Spark-TTS moved to CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/SparkAudio/Spark-TTS-0.5B)). Breeze's weights and self-hosted outputs are research/non-commercial only, and commercial use goes through BreezeBlue's paid API ([HF card](https://huggingface.co/BreezeBlue/Breeze-TTS-2)). The binding constraint is Echo-TTS. Its weights are CC-BY-NC-SA-4.0, and so are its **audio outputs**, because it depends on Fish Speech's S1-DAC autoencoder. Its code is MIT, apart from an Apache-2.0 `autoencoder.py`. The card also forbids mimicking a real person without consent ([jordand/echo-tts-base](https://huggingface.co/jordand/echo-tts-base)). A free app is a non-commercial use, but Yakkinator should tell users plainly that anything Echo renders cannot be used commercially and must be shared under the same license. IndexTTS-2's weight license was not verified in this research, so check its terms in the model repo before making any claim about that path. Since Yakkinator downloads the models at setup, show each model's license in the installer and keep the Apache-2.0 LICENSE/NOTICE files that the Hugging Face download brings along.

## Conclusion

Yakkinator does not need the "best-sounding" open TTS model, only the one that most faithfully turns words into a timbre and then gets out of the way. That makes Qwen3-TTS VoiceDesign the right default even though Breeze sounds better in arenas. The cloner sets final quality, and Qwen is the model that is permissively licensed, easy to install on Windows, and documented for this exact handoff. A side benefit is that designed voices belong to no real person, which avoids the consent problem built into zero-shot cloning. That makes a credible product story, as long as the UI is honest about Echo's NC-SA output terms.

The open questions are empirical, and Yakkinator can answer them better than the literature. No one has published how much speaker similarity survives a Qwen-to-Echo or Qwen-to-IndexTTS-2 handoff, whether English accent prompts are followed, or whether seeds reproduce across GPUs. A small in-app A/B harness can measure all three: design N candidates, clone each through both engines, and compare speaker embeddings. Data from that harness would decide whether VoxCPM2's 48 kHz references or FireRedTTS3's claimed adherence gains justify adding a second designer.
